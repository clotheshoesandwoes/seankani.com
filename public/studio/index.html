Questions

You probably want to know.

  1. Why pay for hand-coded when Wix is twenty dollars a month?

    Wix is fine if your site can be templated. If you want something that doesn't look like the other forty-seven businesses on the same template, it has to be made by hand. There's no plugin for taste.

  2. Do you handle hosting and the domain?

    I deploy your site to Vercel or Netlify, set up the domain, and hand over the keys. Hosting is around zero to ten dollars a month after launch, and that goes to them, not me.

  3. Can I edit content myself afterward?

    Yes. I wire up a simple CMS for any page that should change. Menu, hours, blog. Static stuff stays in code, dynamic stuff you control.

  4. What if I want changes after launch?

    Small tweaks within the first thirty days are free. After that, I bill hourly or we agree on a small retainer. Most clients don't need much.

  5. How long does a project take?

    Two to six weeks. The thirty-minute call up front tells me which. I quote a date and hit it.

  6. Do you do logos or branding?

    No. Websites only. If you need a logo, I can recommend designers I trust.

About

I'm Sean. I live in San Diego.

I've been on the internet my whole life. Old enough to remember when websites let you customize them. When every business had a page that looked like nothing else, made by someone who cared. Most of that is gone now. Most small-business sites today are five Wix templates with a different logo.

I've also worked in sales, support, and customer retention. I've watched, in real time, customers walk away from businesses they were ready to spend money with, because the website didn't load, didn't trust them, or didn't tell them what to do next. The service was fine. The site is what lost the sale.

Bad websites cost you real customers. Usually the ones who were already comfortable, already willing. I build the kind that don't.

Everything is hand-coded. You talk to the person building the site. There is no team to loop in. Three or four clients a quarter, so the work stays at the bar above.
